# 01 — Market Analysis

> Figures below were gathered from live sources (mid-2026) and are cited at the
> bottom. Market-research-firm projections vary widely and should be treated as
> directional, not precise. Re-verify any number before it goes in a pitch deck.

## 1. The category is real, large, and funded

Faith-tech stopped being a curiosity. The anchor data points:

- **YouVersion Bible App** — passed **1 billion installs** (Nov 2025), **100M+
  monthly active users**, available in **2,300 languages**, with a record
  **22.2M people** using it in a single day (Jan 4, 2026). Installs up **~12% YoY**,
  daily use up **~18% YoY**. It is free/donation-funded and effectively owns the
  "read the Bible text" behavior. [1][2]
- **Hallow** (Catholic prayer/meditation) — **22M+ downloads** (Feb 2025), estimated
  **~$51M annual revenue** (May 2025), **$84M+** raised including a **$50M Series C**.
  Freemium at **$9.99/mo or $69.99/yr**. The clearest proof that a *paid
  subscription* faith app can reach real scale. [3][4]
- **Glorify** (devotional/prayer) — **$84.6M** raised (a16z-led **$40M Series A**
  Dec 2021 + **$40M Series B** 2022), celebrity backers. Subscription model. [5]
- **Pray.com** — **$34M+** raised (incl. Kleiner Perkins); daily prayer + bedtime
  Bible stories. [5]
- **Dwell** — audio-Bible subscription with original music/voices; proves people
  will *pay* for a premium audio scripture experience. [5]

**Takeaway:** capital and consumers have both validated paid faith apps. The
white space is not "another prayer app" — those are well funded and entrenched.

## 2. The adjacent markets that fund the "Read" and "Listen" pillars

- **Audiobooks** are the fastest-growing format in publishing. US audiobook
  revenue hit **~$1.1B in 2024, +23.8% YoY** — digital audio (11.3% of the US
  trade market) has now overtaken ebooks. Global audiobook market estimates run
  **~$10B (2025)** with aggressive long-range CAGR projections (firm-dependent,
  treat as directional). [6][7]
- **Ebooks** are flat-to-slow (~1.6% growth, ~10% share) — confirming that the
  *audio* and *subscription* angles matter more than plain ebook sales. [6]
- **Christian publishing** is consolidating around strength — e.g., HarperCollins
  Christian Publishing acquired DaySpring's book/Bible assets (Jan 2026),
  signalling a healthy, acquisitive faith-publishing market. [7]

**Takeaway:** lead with **audio + subscription**, not ebook unit sales. A library
people *listen* to and *subscribe* to is where the momentum is.

## 3. The business-model playbook is mature

The creator-subscription model we'd use is well understood:

- **Substack**: flat **10%** of paid revenue + ~**2.9% + $0.30** processing. Email-
  first, SEO-indexed archive, dead-simple onboarding. [8]
- **Patreon**: **8–12%** + processing (effectively 12–18% all-in), tiered
  memberships, community tools (Discord, comments, multi-format posts). [8]
- Common pattern: **grow with free content, monetize with a paywall + tiers.** [8]

**Takeaway:** we are applying a *proven* model. Our innovation is the **product**
(the answer library + the gift), not the monetization mechanics.

## 4. Where the players sit — and the gap

| Player | Core behavior | Model | What they DON'T do |
|---|---|---|---|
| YouVersion | Read the Bible text | Free/donation | No original long-form answers; no books/podcast marketplace |
| Hallow / Glorify / Pray | Pray, meditate, devotionals | Paid sub | Habit/calm, not *depth*; no Q&A engine |
| Dwell | Listen to scripture | Paid sub | Audio Bible only |
| Logos / Olive Tree | Study/reference (power users) | Buy/sub | Expensive, scholarly, steep curve; not for seekers |
| Audible / Kindle Unlimited | Read/listen broadly | Paid sub | Secular, no community, no answers |
| Substack / Patreon (faith creators) | Read a writer | Paid sub | Single-creator, no library, no structured Q&A, no app |

**The gap we fill:** *No one* combines (a) a **subscription content library**
(books + podcasts) with (b) a **structured, AI-assisted written Q&A engine** that
takes questions seriously and answers them in long form, wrapped in (c) a
**premium, beautiful** product, with (d) a **generosity mechanic** (gift a Bible).
Each piece exists somewhere; the *combination* is unclaimed.

## 5. Audience segments

1. **Seekers / new believers / the doubting** — have real questions, find church
   intimidating, distrust glib answers. The **Ask** pillar is built for them.
   *Highest-value wedge: their questions create the content that attracts the next
   seeker.*
2. **Committed believers wanting depth** — beyond daily-verse apps; want books,
   teaching, and well-reasoned answers. The library subscription is for them.
3. **Givers / patrons** — financially comfortable believers motivated by mission;
   the **Generosity tier + gift-a-Bible** converts them into funders of the free
   tier for everyone else. This is the cross-subsidy that makes the mission work.
4. **Creators / teachers** (later phase) — pastors, writers, podcasters who could
   publish on the platform and share revenue.

## 6. Risks and headwinds (be honest)

- **Incumbent gravity:** YouVersion owns "read scripture"; don't fight it —
  *integrate/complement* (deep-link verses, don't rebuild the Bible reader from
  scratch on day one).
- **Trust & doctrine:** AI-drafted answers on theology are sensitive. A wrong or
  off-doctrine answer is a brand-killer. Mitigation: **human-in-the-loop is
  non-negotiable**, a clearly stated doctrinal stance/statement of faith, and
  visible citations. (See [`05-ai-qa-pipeline.md`](05-ai-qa-pipeline.md).)
- **App-store tax:** Apple/Google take **15–30%** of in-app subscriptions. Plan to
  sell web subscriptions via Stripe and treat the app as access, where store
  rules allow. (See [`07-monetization.md`](07-monetization.md).)
- **Content licensing:** a book/audio library needs rights. Start with
  public-domain + original + a few licensed/partner titles rather than chasing a
  huge catalog you can't afford.
- **Cold-start:** the answer library is empty on day one. Mitigation: seed it with
  a curated set of the most-asked questions answered in advance before launch.

## 7. Strategic conclusion

Don't enter as "another prayer app." Enter as **the place where Christian
questions get real answers, backed by a library worth subscribing to, paid
partly by people who want to give**. Lead with the **Ask** wedge (cheap to build,
uniquely defensible, content-compounding), use **audio + subscription** as the
revenue engine, and use **gift-a-Bible generosity** as the mission and the
marketing story in one.
