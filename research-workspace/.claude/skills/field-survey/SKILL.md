# Field survey

Give her a fast, structured map of a topic so she knows the terrain before reading
deeply. Orientation, not a literature review.

## Inputs
- A topic or the essay question (from `knowledge/current-question.md`).
- `knowledge/sources.md` and `frameworks.md`.

## Steps
1. Search both academic sources and **grey literature** (King's Fund, Nuffield Trust,
   NHS England, etc. — see `sources.md`). Use web search.
2. Synthesise into the map below. Cite real, verifiable sources only; where you're
   unsure a source is real, say so and suggest she verify via the library.

## Output
- **The terrain** — what this field is about, in 3–4 sentences.
- **Key debates** — the live disagreements (the essay should engage these).
- **Schools of thought / main positions** — and who holds them.
- **Seminal works** — the foundational references (author, year, why it matters).
- **Recent developments** — last ~5 years, incl. NHS policy shifts.
- **Gaps & tensions** — where the field is unresolved (fertile ground for her angle).
- **Suggested entry points** — 5–10 sources to read first, prioritised, with a line
  on each. Offer to feed these into `paper-search` / `paper-summary`.

## Rules
- Distinguish peer-reviewed from grey literature.
- Flag her university library as the route to full text.
- Be honest about uncertainty; never fabricate a seminal reference.
