# Theory to practice

The signature skill for a practising senior NHS leader. Helps her connect a framework
to a real situation she has led or observed — **critically**, which is what management
master's assignments reward.

## Inputs
- A theory/model (cross-ref `knowledge/frameworks.md`).
- A real situation from her practice (prompt her for it — service change, staffing/
  retention issue, safety incident, team conflict, reconfiguration…). Held in
  `knowledge/current-question.md` → practice context, or given live.

## Steps
1. **Pin the situation down** — ask her for specifics: what happened, her role,
   stakeholders, constraints, outcome. The richer this is, the better the analysis.
2. **Apply the model** — map its concepts onto the situation. Where does it explain
   what happened well?
3. **Stress-test the fit** — where does the model *fail* to explain it? What does it
   ignore (power, finance, regulation, the clinical/managerial divide)? Bring in the
   model's known critiques from `frameworks.md`.
4. **Bring a second lens** — offer an alternative framework that captures what the
   first missed. Comparing frameworks signals critical depth.
5. **So what** — implications for her practice and for the wider NHS.

## Output
A structured analysis (situation → application → critique → alternative lens →
implications), with Harvard cites for every theoretical claim.

## Rules
- Reflective/first-person passages must be **her own words** — prompt and structure,
  don't write her reflection for her (see workspace integrity rules).
- Never invent details of her situation. Ask.
- Always include the critical/limitations angle — application without critique
  loses marks.
