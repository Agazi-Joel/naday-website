# Citation style

**Default: Harvard — Cite Them Right (UK standard for nursing & management courses).**

> ⚠️ Confirm against the module handbook before relying on this. UK courses vary
> (some business modules use APA 7th). If yours differs, edit this file only — every
> skill reads from here.

## In-text citations

- One author: `(West, 2017)` or `West (2017) argues that…`
- Two authors: `(West and Dawson, 2012)`
- Three or more: `(Bailey et al., 2015)`
- Direct quote: include page number — `(West, 2017, p. 24)`
- Organisation/grey literature: `(The King's Fund, 2022)`, `(NHS England, 2019)`

## Reference list (examples)

**Journal article**
> West, M. (2017) 'Collaborative and compassionate leadership', *Journal of Health
> Organization and Management*, 31(3), pp. 309–315.

**Book**
> Northouse, P.G. (2021) *Leadership: theory and practice*. 9th edn. Thousand Oaks:
> Sage.

**Grey literature / think-tank report**
> The King's Fund (2022) *Leadership in today's NHS*. London: The King's Fund.
> Available at: https://www.kingsfund.org.uk/... (Accessed: 17 June 2026).

**NHS / government source**
> NHS England (2019) *The NHS Long Term Plan*. Available at:
> https://www.longtermplan.nhs.uk/ (Accessed: 17 June 2026).

## Rules skills must follow

- Never invent a reference. Every entry must correspond to a real source the student
  has access to (ideally a file in `papers/3-to-cite/` or a verifiable DOI/URL).
- Grey literature is legitimate and often essential in health management — cite it
  properly with author (organisation), year, title, URL and access date.
- Reference list is built **only** from sources actually cited in the text.
